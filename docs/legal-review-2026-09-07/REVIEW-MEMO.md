## Material revisions

- Distinguish account-based software and licensed professional workspace use from optional human/credit-related support. A label does not create an exemption from credit-repair laws.
- Explicitly state analysis and consultation/review occur before billing, but do not authorize advance collection for future work. Full performance of the particular agreed service and applicable contract/waiting-period requirements still govern.
- Clarify explicit recurring authorization, one-time purchases, cancellation channels and non-waivable refund rights. No promise of unimplemented self-service billing, continuing view-only access or automatic export.
- Explain readiness metrics, user-provided data and AI limitations; no promise of direct bureau access, guaranteed scores, funding or approval.
- Explain professional workspace access and authorized client use; preserve customer ownership of submitted records.
- Remove unsupported blanket privacy compliance, certification, universal seven-year federal retention, WISP and provider zero-retention implications. Describe actual categories and configured-provider dependencies rather than claiming every integration is operational.
- Expand the previously abbreviated statutory credit-file-rights statement in the agreement template. Separate its display from the agreement body; add the cancellation warning in bold near the signature area and purchaser cancellation fields in the saved notice.
- Preserve, qualify rather than newly expand, the existing arbitration/liability provisions. Their enforceability is not decided by these edits.

## Items requiring substantive legal/business resolution before launch sign-off

1. Confirm which actual offers are covered by CROA, state credit-services statutes and any applicable Telemarketing Sales Rule requirements. Reviewing first is not itself an exemption. If the analysis/consultation is a covered service, its start timing also needs review under 15 U.S.C. 1679d.
2. Specify exact purchased services, total payments and performance dates/durations in each signed support agreement. The generic template currently refers to plan/checkout confirmations; it is not proven to satisfy the detailed-contract requirements. Do not invent timelines or segregate a first-work fee merely to justify early collection.
3. Complete and verify separate pre-contract disclosure delivery and signed receipt, duplicate cancellation forms, a consumer copy at signing, retention and E-SIGN consent. A separate UI block and expanded text alone do not prove these statutory formalities are met.
4. Review cancellation-day calculation, holidays, customer timezone and state-specific requirements. Existing runtime excludes weekends and uses server-local date operations; no attorney-approved calendar change was made here.
5. Confirm analysisReview.completedAt records the completed consultation and evidence of all services supporting the actual charge. The existing billing gate remains intact; it is not an independent legal determination that the entire billed service is fully performed.
6. Verify state automatic-renewal requirements and accessible cancellation operations; self-service management is still incomplete. Ensure price/support terms agree with actual checkout and collection practices.
7. Confirm privacy retention schedules, rights operations, AI vendor contracts/training restrictions, actual processor flows, sensitive-data consent, organizational access and security claims. The observed production API lacks BLOB_READ_WRITE_TOKEN; private-storage promises require configuration and an end-to-end test before launch.
8. Review arbitration, liability and education classification under applicable law; the wording does not certify enforceability.
